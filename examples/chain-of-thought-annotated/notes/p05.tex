\block[y=27]{%
\heading{Figure 4: gains appear with scale}
\num{1}\ \textbf{How to read it}: rows = benchmark (GSM8K, SVAMP, MAWPS); columns = model family. $x$ = model size in
billions of parameters (one point per size, evenly spaced); $y$ = solve rate (\%).
\begin{points}
  \item \textbf{black} = standard prompting; \blue{\textbf{blue}} = chain of thought; \orange{dashed} = prior
        supervised best. Look at the top right: PaLM 540B on GSM8K reaches the dashed line.
\end{points}
\begin{keyidea}
The blue curve breaks away from the black one only for the largest models, and most of all on GSM8K.
\end{keyidea}}

\block{%
\num{2}\ \textbf{Takeaway 2: harder problems gain more.} GSM8K (lowest baseline) more than doubled for the largest
GPT and PaLM; on SingleOp (one step) the gains were \hl[hlpink]{negative or very small}.}

\block{%
\num{3}\ \textbf{Takeaway 3: as good as finetuning.} PaLM 540B sets a \hl[hlgreen]{new state of the art} on GSM8K, SVAMP
and MAWPS, and is within 2\,\% on AQuA and ASDiv. On SVAMP, standard prompting had already passed the prior best.}

\block[y=115]{%
\num{4}\ \textbf{Why does it work? They read the chains} (LaMDA 137B, GSM8K):
\begin{smalltable}{l l}
50 correct answers & all chains sound, \textbf{except 2} (right by luck) \\
50 wrong answers & \red{\textbf{46\%}} almost right (a calculator slip, a missing step) \\
 & \red{\textbf{54\%}} major errors (understanding, coherence) \\
\end{smalltable}
Scaling PaLM from 62B to 540B fixes most one-step-missing and semantic errors (Appendix A.1).}

\block[y=203]{%
\num{5}\ \textbf{§3.3 Ablation}: is it really the chain of thought, or something that comes with it? Three variants,
each removing one ingredient (Figure 5, next page).}

\block[y=224]{%
\num{6}\ \textbf{Equation only}: write just the equation, then the answer. It \hl[hlpink]{does not help much on GSM8K}:
those questions are too hard to turn straight into an equation. It does help on one- or two-step datasets.}

\block{%
\begin{selftest}
On which benchmark did chain of thought help most, and what does that tell you?
\answer{GSM8K, the hardest (lowest baseline): it more than doubled for the largest GPT and PaLM, while one-step
SingleOp barely moved. The more steps a problem needs, the more the chain helps.}
\end{selftest}}
