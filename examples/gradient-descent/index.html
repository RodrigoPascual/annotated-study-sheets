Part 1Follow the slope down

If you are blindfolded on a hill, how do you reach the valley?

# Part 1 ex: blindfolded on a hill, you feel the ground with your feet and step where it goes down. That is all gradient descent does. → The loss landscape.

Walking downhill on the loss

  • The loss says how wrong the model is.
  • The gradient is the slope: it points uphill.
  • Step the other way: downhill.
# The loss landscape ! The gradient points uphill, so we step in the opposite direction. (1) Horizontal axis: one parameter w. Vertical: the loss L(w), how wrong the model is. - The orange dots are successive steps. Look how they get closer together near the bottom. - That is automatic: the slope is smaller there, so the step is smaller. (2) Loss = a single number summarising the errors (e.g. mean squared error). (3) Gradient = derivative of the loss with respect to each parameter. - In 1D it is just the slope; with millions of parameters it is a vector with one slope per parameter. warn: the gradient is local. It only tells you about the ground under your feet, not where the global minimum is. → The update rule.

The whole algorithm is one line

w ← w − η · ∇L(w)
  • w: the parameters (weights).
  • η: the learning rate, the size of the step.
  • ∇L(w): the gradient of the loss.
  • Repeat until the loss stops falling.
# The update rule ! New weights = old weights minus a small step in the direction of the gradient. (1) Read it right to left: compute the slope, scale it by η, subtract it. f: w_{t+1} = w_{t} − η · ∇L(w_{t}) (2) w can be one number or millions of them; the rule is identical. (3) η (eta) is a hyper-parameter: we choose it, the model does not learn it. (4) The minus sign is the whole trick: gradient = uphill, minus gradient = downhill. ?? Why not jump straight to the minimum? Because we do not know where it is; we only know the local slope. → How big should the step be?

The learning rate decides everything

# Learning rate ! Too small wastes time; too big never converges. Tuning η is the first thing to try when training misbehaves. (1) Tiny steps: the loss goes down, but you need thousands of iterations. (2) A good η: big steps where the slope is steep, small ones near the bottom. (3) Too big: each step jumps over the valley to a higher point on the other side, and the loss explodes. - Symptom in practice: the loss becomes NaN or grows epoch after epoch. | η | Behaviour | |---|---| | 0.0001 | slow | | 0.01 | converges | | 1.0 | diverges | ex: typical starting values are 0.001 for Adam and 0.01–0.1 for plain SGD; always check on your own problem. → Can we do better than fixed steps?

Part 2Better than plain steps

What if the valley is long, narrow and noisy?

# Part 2 - Real loss surfaces are not smooth bowls: they have ravines, plateaus and noise from mini-batches. → Momentum and Adam.

Momentum and Adam

OptimizerWhat it addsIntuition
SGDGradient of a mini-batchFeel the slope, step
MomentumRunning average of past gradientsA ball that keeps rolling
AdamMomentum + per-parameter step sizeEach weight gets its own η

Adam is the usual default; plain SGD with momentum often generalises slightly better when tuned.

Kingma & Ba (2015), Adam: A Method for Stochastic Optimization.

# Momentum and Adam ! Momentum smooths the direction; Adam also adapts the step size of every parameter. (1) Three rows, each adding one idea to the previous one. - SGD: “stochastic” because each step uses a random mini-batch, not the whole dataset. - Momentum: keeps a velocity, so noise cancels out and narrow ravines are crossed faster. - Adam: divides each step by a running estimate of the gradient’s size → big steps for rarely-updated weights, small for noisy ones. > Kingma & Ba (2015) introduce Adam: momentum on the gradient plus a running average of its square. (2) A fair caveat, not a rule. ?? Which one should I use? Start with Adam (η ≈ 0.001); if you have time to tune, compare with SGD + momentum. → Takeaways.

What to remember

  1. Gradient = uphill. Step the other way.
  2. η is the step size: the first knob to turn.
  3. Momentum and Adam make the walk smoother and faster.
# Takeaways (1) One line of maths: w ← w − η·∇L(w). (2) Loss exploding? Lower η. Loss crawling? Raise it. (3) Default: Adam with η ≈ 0.001. → Questions.
